What it does

Every tracked item carries an id in its NBT and a record of where it is, so ItemGuard can find it, recover it or rebuild it later. Sealed items refuse to be dropped, picked up by strangers, sucked into hoppers, mined out or destroyed by explosions. It never drops items on the ground and never duplicates them.

Core ideas

Six concepts cover everything the plugin does.

Templates

A template names a group of items and carries the flags they share. Every item inherits them; each can override any single flag.

Templates →

Flags

Seven behavioural switches with conservative defaults: drop, pickup, move, hopper transfer, break, explosion and recovery.

Flags →

Seals & reclaim

A dropped item gets a bedrock seat and a floating label. The owner takes it back with /ig reclaim — proven, limited, recorded.

Seals →

In-game screens

Every item has a record on screen: its state, its owner, where it was last seen, its flags and its whole history.

The screens →

One database per server

SQLite out of the box, MySQL or PostgreSQL when you outgrow a file. Enforced at start-up so two servers can never act on the wrong world.

Database →

Nine languages

Every message is editable in its own file with documented placeholders, and keys a version adds still work after an upgrade.

Translations →

Requirements

Paper 1.21+ and Java 21. That is the whole list — the database drivers are inside the jar, and LuckPerms and SmartSpawner are optional, never required.
